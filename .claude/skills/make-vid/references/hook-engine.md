# Hook engine

Condensed from the Hook Master suite by Liam Johnston (https://github.com/Liamrjohnston/hook-master-skill, MIT licence, copy in HOOK-MASTER-LICENSE.txt). Rewritten for Chalkboard's context. Run all stages silently and show only the result in the card.

A hook has two jobs: make the topic clear at once, and make the right viewer curious about something this video actually answers. Clever but unclear fails. Clear but obvious fails. The footage must pay it off within the next few seconds.

## 1. Four failures

Quote the current opening (spoken words, on-screen text, first shot). For each, PASS or FAIL with the exact words or frame.

| Failure | Test | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Delay | Words or seconds before a stranger knows the topic. Greetings, "so", "welcome to Chalkboard" | Start on the first word that carries the subject. Move the payoff frame to second zero |
| Confusion | Could a tired stranger say back the topic after one listen? | One meaning, plain verbs, name the thing, no school jargon |
| Irrelevance | Is it about Chalkboard, not the parent? Logo, "about us" | Lead with the parent's worry or want. Chalkboard comes later as proof |
| Disinterest | Is there a gap between what the parent believes and what the video shows? | Add contrast: old way versus this way, the worry versus the fix |

A vague spoken line is fine if the picture or text carries the topic. Check all three channels together.

## 2. Viewer panel (thinking tool, not data)

Each viewer sees only the first 3 seconds and says SWIPE or STAY with a reason tied to something on screen or in the words.

- Doom-scroller: thumb moving, sound maybe off.
- Sceptic: smells clickbait and unbacked claims. The honesty alarm.
- Beginner: new parent, doesn't know board or syllabus terms.
- Niche expert: a teacher or an experienced parent who knows the basics.
- Buyer: a parent who could book an assessment this week.
- Rival: another tuition centre's marketer.

For Chalkboard the buyer and the beginner matter most. Do not let all six agree by default. Never present the panel as a survey.

## 3. Council

Turn the verdicts into three ranked changes with exact before and after words. Rank by who it keeps (buyer and beginner count double), whether the footage can pay it off, and cost (cut or reorder, then text, then reshoot). Tie-break order: delay, confusion, irrelevance, disinterest. Add one "Not doing" line for the tempting change that loses.

## 4. Six formats

First write the contrast: "Parents think [A]. This video shows [B]." Then rewrite in formats that the real footage supports:

| Format | Shape | Chalkboard example shape |
|---|---|---|
| Fortune Teller | Soon / next time you [situation], [what changes] | "Next time your child says maths is boring, try this." |
| Experimenter | I tried [x]. [Surprising part] | Only if the team really ran it, with footage |
| Teacher | How to [result] without [the thing they hate] | "How to revise Class 9 science without cramming." |
| Investigator | Why [problem], and it's not [usual guess] | "Why your child forgets what they studied, and it's not laziness." |
| Contrarian | Stop [habit]. [What works] | Must be fair. No straw man, no attack on schools or other tutors |
| Magician | A striking first frame, layered on another format | The chalkboard wiped, a problem solved in one shot |

Drop any format that needs proof Chalkboard doesn't have. A question is a hook only if the viewer can't answer "no" and swipe.

## 5. Niche tuner (local tuition)

- Viewer in one line: "[Parent of a Class N child in Bengaluru], who [situation], and wants [result] or fears [problem]."
- Use parent words only if the brief quotes them. Your own phrase is yours, never put it in quotes or say parents say it.
- Size the stakes truthfully: a pending chapter, a nervous board year.
- Proof that lands: what happens in a class, the batch cap, the location, the real hours. Avoid outcome promises, "best in Bangalore", ranks.

## 6. Visual match

The first shot shows the subject of the sentence. Pick the frame that best teases the payoff from footage that exists (or a graphic or generated non-human shot), put it at second zero, and prefer movement (a hand writing, chalk dust, a page turning). On-screen text, 3 to 7 words, adds to the spoken line. Test with sound off, then eyes closed.

## 7. Lock-in

The 1 to 3 sentences after the hook confirm its promise. Pick one move: proof right away, picture the result, stack a second contrast, name the method, answer the objection, or say it concretely. Cut any intro, backstory or "before we start". The Chalkboard story comes after the payoff, as proof.
